12 · The ask

Six actions still open to this court

Revised September 2026 — the asks that mattered in June have been adopted. These are what is left.

The June version of this slide asked the court to refuse abatements and to make data centers a standing public agenda item. It did both — Resolution 26-25 and the June 9 water resolution are on the record, and the evening sessions happened. What follows is not a repeat. Each item below is something no one has done yet, none require new statutory authority, and none run through the land-use route that has cost Hill County and Hood County so dearly.

In fairness to this court: campaign-finance filings show no contributions to any member — Judge Deen or Commissioners Conley, Holt, Walden, or Hale — from Black Mountain, Fort Worth Power Core, or Bennett. That is not an assumption: all 234 campaign-finance reports posted by Parker County for 25 elected officials, 2021–2026, were pulled and machine-read, and searched against 30 entity patterns covering the Black Mountain ecosystem and 27 other data-center actors. Zero matches in any donor field. The 22 surface hits were all expenditures or false positives — including a payee named Jeff Bennett, no relation, and a street called Bennett Road. The documented giving went to state and city officials, not to this county. This request is not an accusation.
1. Send the TCEQ letter, and pair it with a records request. A letter to the Office of Air Quality has been drafted and is ready for the clerk. It asks the one question the record cannot answer: Parker County sits in the DFW Severe ozone nonattainment area, where the major-source NOx threshold is 25 tons per year, not 250 — was Registration 179422 evaluated against that threshold, and against the continuous operating profile testified to on May 26, rather than the "backup/bridge power" characterization in the application? Send with it a public-information request for the full registration file. The permitted capacity of this plant does not appear on any public surface. The Central Registry entry has no capacity; the Central File Room returns no documents. That number should not be something a citizen has to infer.
2. Amend the June 9 water resolution to cover total system water. As written it opposes open-loop evaporative cooling. That is right but incomplete: a closed-loop hall drawing combined-cycle or grid power consumes about 205 million gallons a year at 75 MW — essentially the same as an evaporative hall, just consumed where the electricity is made. The resolution should state the county's standard as total water per unit of computing — cooling plus generation, so that a project cannot satisfy it by moving the water upstream.
3. Put Parker County on the record with the Upper Trinity GCD. Three specific requests: a standing request for notice of every high-volume permit application filed anywhere in the county; a stated intent to seek a contested-case hearing if one is filed for this tract; and a request that the district require whole-system water disclosure — cooling and on-site generation together — in any high-volume application. The district's rules are the one authority in this picture that HB 2127 did not preempt, and the district is already building a local groundwater flow model that will settle the thickness question at this tract.
4. File with the PUCT and ERCOT before the audit closes. Every data center in the interconnection queue must now report its incentives, power draw, water use, community mitigation and ownership; ERCOT is working to finish by December 10, 2026. Parker County should ask, in writing, for two things: that results be published at county level rather than aggregated statewide, and that water be reported as a whole-system figure. The RFI currently collects cooling technology and power sourcing in separate boxes — nobody upstream is adding them together.
5. Settle the Chapter 391 question with a date certain. Either route is defensible — a Parker–Hood sub-regional commission, or working inside the North Central Texas COG, which is itself a 391. What is not defensible is the drift this court named in open session on July 13: a meeting in August to decide what to discuss in September. Direct the County Judge to return a recommendation and a draft interlocal agreement by a specific date, and to open contact with the other counties where this operator holds air permits — Bell, Bowie, Carson, Cherokee, Fannin, Hale, Jack, Somervell, Tarrant, Wheeler and Williamson.
6. Extend the no-abatement position to the units it does not reach, and set the terms for what does get built. Resolution 26-25 binds neither a future court nor the hospital district, junior college, ESD-1, or lateral road fund — each grants its own Chapter 312 agreement. Ask each to adopt the same position. And adopt now, before construction traffic, a policy requiring a road-use and infrastructure agreement with financial assurance as a condition of any development at this scale. That is an ordinary county power, it survives the preemption cases, and it is far easier to require before a project breaks ground than after.

None of these require new statutory authority. Items 1 and 4 are letters; 2 and 6 are resolutions; 3 is a filing with a district the county already sits inside; 5 is a scheduling decision. What is deliberately absent: any moratorium, plat denial, or land-use prohibition — the routes that produced a $100 million claim against Hill County and litigation against Hood County under Civil Practice & Remedies Code Ch. 102A.
