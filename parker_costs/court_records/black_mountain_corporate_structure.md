## The TCEQ permit (Parker site, RN112172408)

- **Type: Air New Source Registration #179422** (NOT a full NSR permit — a Standard Permit / Permit-by-Rule)
- **Site name: "PARKER PLANT"** at 501 Pearson Ranch Rd
- **Status: ACTIVE**
- **Owner/operator: Fort Worth Power Core LLC**, since **03/13/2025**
- The Standard Permit type explains the 2.5-week turnaround Hotopp testified to — it's a fast-track template, not the months-long NSR process
- **However:** Standard Permits in TX typically *still* require some public notice under 30 TAC §39.418-.420. The "no public process" claim Hotopp made on the record means either:
  1. The notice happened in a venue the city didn't track (TCEQ publication, not local newspaper)
  2. The registration was eligible for *no-notice* PBR-style treatment due to the equipment fitting standard authorization conditions
  3. Some procedural irregularity occurred

The actual permit file (FOIA-able from TCEQ Information Resources) would resolve this.

### Patterns worth noting

- **Hill County is absent.** Hill passed the moratorium that triggered the Bettencourt letter. Fort Worth Power Core didn't pursue it.
- **Hood County is absent.** Hood declined a data-center plat. Fort Worth Power Core didn't pursue it.
- **Hays County is absent.** Hays has active organizing. Fort Worth Power Core didn't pursue it.
- **Williamson County (Jonah) IS in the list.** Williamson hasn't moved on moratoriums yet; per Sid Miller's commentary, this is a county worth alerting.
- **Somervell County (Glen Rose)** is immediately adjacent to Hood. Hood's moratorium may have *pushed* the developer one county west — which would be a model story for what happens when one county resists and the neighboring county doesn't.
- **Three Tarrant County entries are "SIEMENS SGT-800"** — that's a specific 50–60 MW industrial gas-turbine model. Fort Worth Power Core has registered three of them in Tarrant. These could be the equipment registrations associated with adjacent plant sites — or they could indicate Fort Worth Power Core is operating *behind-the-meter* generation for *existing* Fort Worth data centers (the DFW colo cluster).
- **Bell County (Temple)** has a Microsoft data center already. Now Fort Worth Power Core is adding a gas plant there too. Same pattern as Parker — gas plant supplying a DC.

## What this means for the Parker case

1. **The 75 MW Parker site is not a one-off** — it's one of 14 sites in a systematic statewide rollout. Black Mountain / Fort Worth Power Core has been quietly registering gas plants across Texas since at least early 2025.
2. **The Standard Permit / Permit-by-Rule pathway explains the 2.5-week timeline** — it's a legitimate fast-track, not a violation. But it almost certainly means the public-notice requirement was minimal or absent.
3. **The Parker site has been registered to Fort Worth Power Core since March 13, 2025** — 14 months before Hotopp's testimony at the May 26 commissioners court. The county and city were not informed because they were not procedurally entitled to be.
4. **Rhett Bennett's Black Mountain Group is an oil-and-gas operator getting into the data-center buildout** — the EDC Director's "oil and gas company" characterization is confirmed.
5. **The 14-site rollout is the real story** — Parker is the visible piece. Williamson, Bell, Somervell, Cherokee, Carson, etc. are the parallel cases. Whatever Parker decides on incentives sets the precedent for the other 13.
