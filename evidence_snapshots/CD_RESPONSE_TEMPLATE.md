## Internal notes (do not send)

### What demands look like and how to read them

- **Vague "this is defamatory" letters** with no specific factual error identified → typical opening move; the response above invites them to identify specifics. Often results in no follow-up.
- **Specific factual challenges** → engage on the merits. If wrong, correct. If right, document and respond with the source.
- **Demands to take down "trade secret" or "confidential" information** → none of the cited material is confidential. All Texas tax-rate, parcel-appraisal, TCEQ permit, and SOS-registry data is public by statute.
- **Demands that name you personally and threaten litigation** → save the letter. The threat itself, especially if combined with content-based objections, strengthens an anti-SLAPP defense if they file.

### Texas anti-SLAPP — quick orientation

- **Statute:** Tex. Civ. Prac. & Rem. Code Ch. 27 (Texas Citizens Participation Act).
- **Coverage:** Speech, petition, association on matters of public concern.
- **Mechanism:** Defendant files a TCPA motion to dismiss within 60 days of service.
- **Standard:** Plaintiff must establish by clear-and-specific evidence each element of the claim. Defendant can then offer affirmative defenses.
- **Fee-shifting:** If TCPA motion succeeds, court SHALL award reasonable attorney's fees and costs to the defendant, plus sanctions sufficient to deter similar actions.
- **Practical:** TCPA-experienced counsel filing under §27.003 within 60 days is the standard defensive posture if a SLAPP suit is filed.

### Don't do these things

- Do **not** delete content from the repository in response to a demand. If a specific factual correction is warranted, AMEND with a dated revision note. Deletion looks like consciousness of guilt.
- Do **not** respond informally or by phone before any written engagement. Get the demand in writing first; respond in writing.
- Do **not** name additional persons or expand factual claims in the response. Stick to what's already published.
- Do **not** apologize. Acknowledge that any specific factual error will be corrected on its merits. That is the correct posture; "I'm sorry for the inconvenience" reads as admission.
